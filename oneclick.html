The workflow problem

Independent clinicians still run a practice across calendars, notes, billing, and messages that do not share a coherent workflow. The cost is time spent on administration rather than on the work they trained for.

OneClick is administrative software. It is not clinical decision support and is not intended to replace professional clinical judgment.

Ownership

I co-founded OneClick and lead the technical work: customer discovery, clinician co-design, product decisions, architecture, engineering, cloud infrastructure, security, integrations, deployment, and operations. The work has been built alongside CAE employment.

How clinician feedback changes the product

Decisions come from conversations, testing, and co-design with practicing clinicians. That feedback changes which workflows we build first, how screens are sequenced, and which automations are worth the engineering cost.

Specific named examples stay off this page until they are cleared for public use. The method is documented; individual clinic stories are not.

Architecture, in public

A compact stack used to ship and operate the product:

  • Django and REST APIs
  • PostgreSQL
  • Azure containers
  • Stripe for payments
  • AWS SES for email

One substantive decision: keep the product an operations system with a conventional web architecture, rather than wrapping a language model around clinical work. Automation belongs in administration. Anything that would imply clinical AI stays out.
