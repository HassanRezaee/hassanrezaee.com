Founder case

OneClick

Practice-management software for mental-health professionals.

Co-founder & Technical Lead · 2024–present · Launch planned October 2026

Visit OneClick →

The operational problem

Independent mental-health professionals often manage scheduling, billing, receipts, reminders, and client information across disconnected tools and repetitive administrative workflows.

OneClick is administrative software for that work. It does not replace professional clinical judgment.

How a week of practice actually moves

  1. Scheduleavailability and bookings
  2. Sessionthe appointment happens
  3. Receipt / invoicegenerated from the calendar
  4. Deliverysend one, or a week at once
  5. Reportingpractice operations, not clinical notes

What the product does

  • Scheduling and booking confirmations
  • Receipts and invoicing
  • Reminders and client communication
  • Client information
  • Billing and expensing
  • Secure delivery
  • Practice reporting
  • Authentication and privacy controls

Responsibilities

I co-founded OneClick and lead technical delivery: customer discovery, clinician co-design, product decisions, architecture, full-stack engineering, cloud infrastructure, security, integrations, deployment, and day-to-day operations. The product has been built alongside CAE employment, not as a replacement for it.

A decision that came from clinicians

Co-design sessions — seven rounds with 38 mental-health professionals, plus input from 12 psychologists — kept returning to the same load: receipts after every session, clicked one by one. The product therefore generates receipts and invoices from the calendar, reuses details from previous sessions, and lets a clinician review once and send a week of receipts in a single action.

That is why OneClick stayed on scheduling, billing, receipts, delivery, and reporting, rather than becoming a clinical-notes product.
