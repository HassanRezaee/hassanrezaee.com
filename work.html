CAE · Data Scientist · 2022–present

Enterprise agentic workflows

User and problem

Internal manufacturing stakeholders needed systems that could use proprietary knowledge and interact with existing operational software, rather than leaving people to stitch answers together by hand.

Responsibility and collaborators

I work across problem framing, system design, implementation, evaluation, and iteration with domain experts, architects, data scientists, data engineers, and software engineers. The official role remains Data Scientist; the work includes technical leadership inside those collaborations.

System approach

Agentic workflows that retrieve proprietary knowledge, call deterministic tools, keep structured state, and return outputs for human review or downstream systems. Implementation spans model behaviour, application logic, data access, cloud deployment, and the user workflow around the system.

Decision

Treat the language model as one component, not the system. Tools, retrieval, and approval boundaries carry the parts of the workflow that must stay deterministic or reviewable.

Evaluation and feedback

Evaluation includes whether the system retrieves the right information, uses tools correctly, respects workflow constraints, and produces outputs that domain experts can actually use. Failure analysis and user review shape the next iteration.

Outcome and stage

Active internal engineering. Integration and deployment continue with existing enterprise systems. Public description stays qualitative because the workflows and data are proprietary.

OneClick · Co-founder & Technical Lead · 2024–present

Practice-management software with clinicians

User and problem

Independent mental-health professionals still depend on disconnected tools and repetitive administrative steps that do not match how they actually work.

Responsibility and collaborators

I own customer discovery, clinician co-design, product decisions, architecture, full-stack engineering, infrastructure, security, integrations, deployment, and operations. The product is built with practicing clinicians. This work has been developed alongside CAE employment; it is not described here as full-time employment.

Product approach

OneClick brings everyday practice-management workflows into a single administrative platform. Software automation reduces repetitive work. The product is not intended to replace professional clinical judgment.

Decision

Keep the system administrative. Features are shaped by clinician feedback on workflow and usability rather than by adding clinical decision support.

Evaluation and feedback

Repeated conversations, testing, and co-design with clinicians change workflow design, feature priorities, and technical trade-offs.

Outcome and stage

Pre-launch. Preparing for launch.

CAE · supporting case

Predictive maintenance and forecasting

User and problem

Operational teams needed machine-learning systems for demand forecasting and predictive maintenance from complex time-series and equipment data.

Responsibility

I designed, trained, validated, and helped put models into production workflows with domain experts and engineering counterparts.

Approach

Deep-learning predictive maintenance, demand forecasting, large-scale data processing, scheduled inference, monitoring, and integration with operational systems. Related earlier CAE work included speech and language-model systems for noisy environments and technical documents.

Evaluation

Validation, monitoring, and iteration with the people who used the forecasts and maintenance signals.

Stage

Completed production machine-learning work that precedes the current agentic systems.
